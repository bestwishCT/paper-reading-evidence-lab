19: Instead, we leverage the original answers to gen-
20: erate sub-claims and use them to serve as an esti-
21: mate of the different aspects of the answers that
22: we expect the model to cover. This approach is
23: inspired by works in summarization evaluation and
24: claim verification (Zhang and Bansal, 2021; Kamoi
25: et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2020).
26: Specifically, we usetext-davinci-003 to gen-
27: erate the sub-claims. We first manually annotate
28: three question and answer pairs from the original
29: ELI5 training set with 3 sub-claims each. Then,
30: we prompt text-davinci-003 with these pairs as
31: demonstrations. The full prompt with an example
32: is shown in Table 22.
33: InstructGPT generates coherent and faithful
34: sub-claims. To ensure that the generated sub-
35: claims are of good quality, we manually inspect
36: a random sample of 40 answers and their generated
37: sub-claims (totaling to 120 sub-claims). For each
38: sub-claim, we assign a score of 1 if it is relevant to
39: the question and faithful to the facts presented in
40: the ground truth, and 0 otherwise. We found that
41: 112 out of the 120 (93.33%) sub-claims received a
42: score of 1, meaning that our generated sub-claims
43: are of high quality and faithful to the ground truth.
44: Furthermore, the average number of words in the
45: generated sub-claims is 14 words, and they are
46: typically just one sentence long. This is aligned
47: with the intent behind the metric—to capture short
48: factual claims made by the original answer.
49: NLI model accurately predicts the entailment
50: of sub-claims. We further analyze our sub-claim
51: evaluation metrics by checking the error rate of
52: the final prediction of the NLI model. To this end,
53: we first manually annotate the entailment scores
54: between 40 outputs and their sub-claims (in total
55: of 120 pairs; these are the same questions from the
56: previous analysis). We then use the NLI model
57: to obtain the entailment scores for the output and
58: sub-claims. Using the human annotations as the
59: ground truth label, we found that the NLI model
60: achieved an accuracy of 80.0%.
